The queue workflow can offer real value when serving state is reliable. Students praised clean and straightforward use (4, 6, 12, 23), access to several TAs (14, 15, 22), and course information and scores (17). Student 16 liked the initiative and student 23 found task sign-offs straightforward once located. Four of six TAs found the queue more efficient (3, 4, 6, 7), all six rated the status dropdowns 4 or 5, and five rated projector layout 4 or 5 despite less frequent use. Student 11 expressed broad dissatisfaction alongside frustration with staffing. Student 17 also asked for more TAs. Availability of staff is an operational dimension.

## Overlaps in this sample

- Three of nine students who rated queue/progress ease 1–2 reported rarely or never getting the serving notification. Seven of 14 who gave higher ease scores reported the same issue. The notification problem cut across ease ratings.
- Low ease ratings were 5/18 in Clifton and 4/5 in Bahadurabad. Rarely/never alerts were 8/18 and 2/5 respectively. The five-person group supports a follow-up question about local workflow and network conditions.
- Both TAs who reported accept/call issues (2, 5) also reported reduced workflow efficiency and crashes, lag or unexpected behavior. Two of the four others also reported unexpected behavior. All six answered “No” to the disconnect/sync question, while all six rated serving notifications as less than always.
- Overall experience was 4 or 5 for 15/23 students and 3/6 TAs. Nine students rated combined queue joining/progress tracking at 1–2, while six reported loading, freezing or errors. The combined question cannot identify which of the two activities caused the low score.

These are descriptive overlaps in small, self-selected groups. Ratings are ordinal, and live-report evidence is unavailable. No causal or population-level claims follow.

## Feature requests by row

- Student 4 requested assignment instructions in Kairos. Existing LMS assignments and lessons can centralize links and instructions. Verify how the audited course uses them.
- Students 6 and 15 requested clearer alerts and student 16 a less crowded mobile layout. These fit existing workflows.
- Student 14 requested in-app calls, student 17 a mobile app, and student 22 direct code submission. Review existing links, responsive flows, and assignments before expanding the product.
- TA 2 requested requeue without losing position and a way to sign off outside the queue. Permission, ordering and recovery semantics need a product decision. Existing viewing and signing code is course- and enrollment-scoped independently of serving.
- TA 3 requested calls to notify TAs and a competitive marking system. The approved task rating is course-defined operational feedback. A leaderboard or selection workflow would need separate approval.
- TA 5 asked for manager-like recovery permissions. Investigate and fix inconsistent serving state first. New recovery authority requires explicit scope and audit rules.
- TA 7 requested light projector display. Revisit the existing projector surface and compare room visibility before proposing a larger redesign.
